RESEARCH · VISUAL SPEECH RECOGNITION

Support-conditioned LoRA for speaker adaptation

Cashel Fitzgerald and Jihyun Kim investigated how a lip-reading model can adapt to an unfamiliar speaker using a short, unlabeled video prefix. A hypernetwork predicts LoRA adapter weights, allowing the frozen Auto-AVSR model to adapt without gradient updates at inference time.

By Cashel Fitzgerald · Research with Jihyun Kim · Cornell Tech

The problem

Visual speech recognition predicts words from lip motion. A model’s accuracy can change with a speaker’s articulation, camera angle, or recording conditions. The research asks whether a few frames at the start of a clip can provide enough information to personalize a pretrained model without a separate fine-tuning process.

Our approach

We kept the Auto-AVSR backbone frozen and introduced low-rank adapters into selected encoder layers. Features from the opening frames condition a hypernetwork, which produces the adapter weights before the full video is decoded.

The paper describes our PyTorch Lightning implementation, adapter placement, conditioning design, and training experiments. Adaptation uses the video prefix without speaker identity labels, additional support clips, or test-time gradient updates.

Reported results

On the LRS2 evaluation described in the paper, word error rate decreased from 51.5% for the unadapted baseline to 49.4%. That is a 2.1 percentage-point decrease, or approximately a 4.1% relative reduction. The hypernetwork used approximately 25.6 million parameters.

What did not work, and what remains open

The improvement depended on substantial hypernetwork capacity. Short prefixes contain limited speaker information, and smaller conditioning models did not provide the same gains. An earlier attempt to train a VALLR-style model from scratch suffered from CTC blank collapse, producing mostly empty transcriptions.

These results apply to the configuration and dataset evaluated in the paper. They do not establish comparable gains on other speakers, datasets, or backbones. Longer support sequences and more efficient conditioning are directions for further work.

Read the paper

The full report contains the architecture, experiment details, negative results, and references. It is coauthored by Cashel Fitzgerald and Jihyun Kim.
